# Evidence corpus

Three controlled captures taken 2026-10-02. Same paragraph, same speaker, same microphone
(macOS built-in, index 2), same recording pipeline. Only Wispr Flow's **Auto Cleanup** level
changed between runs.

| Run | Auto Cleanup | Scratchpad note | Survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| `none` | None | `df275e8f` | **96.9%** |
| `light` | Light (**product default**) | `c76b268c` | **60.0%** |
| `medium` | Medium | `40458f96` | **56.9%** |

Spoken ground truth: 65 tokens. Light loses 36.9 points against None; Medium loses 40.0.
The two are indistinguishable at this sample size.

## What died at the default

Present in the audio, absent from the delivered text at **both** Light and Medium:

- `score.py` — the target filename
- `under 200 lines` — a numeric constraint
- `Oh and actually scratch the line limit` — the mid-sentence retraction

`Levenshtein` survives Light but is misspelled `levstein` at Medium. Backticks are inserted at
Medium and nowhere else.

The pattern: **prose survives, specifications do not.** Filler words and connective tissue pass
through untouched. Filenames, magic numbers, and corrections are what disappear.

## Honest limitations

These are real constraints on how far this evidence goes. Do not let anyone quote these numbers
without them.

1. **n = 1 per bar.** One utterance, one speaker, one session. This is a demonstration that the
   effect exists and is large, not a benchmark. The `expected` survival ranges in `corpus.json`
   are deliberately wide for this reason.

2. **Pace was not matched.** Audio durations differed substantially between runs (`medium` 317s,
   `none` 119s, `light` 36s after silence trimming). A faster or more emphatic reading could
   plausibly change which constraints survive. This is the most likely confound and it is not
   controlled.

3. **Surface A is reconstructed by a different engine.** Whisper did not produce the audio, so
   "what was spoken" here is the *script*, and the local Whisper transcript is a separate,
   imperfect reading of the same audio. Whisper and Wispr disagreed in both directions: Whisper
   heard `deflib` where Wispr heard `difflib` correctly, and Whisper heard `lavasting` where
   Wispr wrote `levstein`. Neither engine is reliably better, which is itself the argument for
   measuring rather than reasoning about which words get lost.

4. **Audio is excluded from git.** The WAVs are large and, more importantly, they contain a
   voice. The text surfaces are committed because the scorer operates on those. Reproduce with
   the procedure below.

5. **One marker word leaked into two runs.** `end utterance` appears at the tail of the `none`
   and `light` notes and inflated the denominator slightly. It is stripped before scoring.

## Reproducing

1. Wispr Flow → Style → Auto Cleanup → set the level.
2. Open Scratchpad, start a new note, click into the body.
3. Hold `fn`, read `spoken_ground_truth` aloud at a natural pace, release.
4. Hold `fn`, say `end utterance`, release.
5. Record the microphone concurrently:
   `ffmpeg -f avfoundation -i ":2" -ar 16000 -ac 1 captures/run.wav`
6. Read the delivered text back via the Wispr MCP `search_scratchpad_notes` / `get_scratchpad_note`.
7. Repeat for each level.

Match pace across runs. The corpus above did not, and that is limitation 2.

## Why this corpus exists in the repo

The published research on voice-to-agent prompting measures accuracy cost under synthetic
perturbations of already-written prompts. It does not measure a developer's actual speech losing
its specifications. That gap is what this fills, and the corpus is the proof the gap is real
rather than asserted. It is the only evidence in the project that was not generated by the
project itself.
